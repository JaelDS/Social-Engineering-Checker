University work-experience project

CheckFirst

A calmer next step when a message, link or phone call feels wrong.

Social engineering guidance for everyday users
Company and community pilot proposal · September 2026

01

CheckFirst is a university work-experience project designed for people who do not work in cybersecurity. It helps someone pause, examine warning signs and choose a sensible next step. This is a prototype and a proposal for a supervised pilot. No company, university endorsement, customer deployment or measured impact is claimed.

The user problem

Pressure arrives in familiar places

An urgent email.
A delivery text.
A convincing caller.
A QR code at the wrong moment.

People need an understandable explanation before they respond.

02

These are illustrative situations, not a prevalence claim. A message can borrow a familiar name, add urgency and ask the recipient to act before checking. CheckFirst provides a place to stop and examine the request. The intended audiences include students, staff, community members and people supporting less technical relatives.

03

The five input modes are links, messages, email files, phone numbers and QR images. A QR image is decoded locally so its content can be examined without opening the destination. Phone interpretation includes country clarification. The value is an understandable explanation and a route to independent verification. CheckFirst uses local rules to highlight features such as pressure and unusual link structure. Results must be read as guidance. An ordinary-looking message can still be harmful. A warning sign can also appear in legitimate communication.

A phone country helps interpret number format, but cannot establish who is calling. The application cannot certify a link or verify the identity of a sender. Privacy by design

Analysis starts on the user’s device

Within the browser

Local analysis of submitted content.

No application database.

No account required to try the tool.

Before sharing a report

Use the redacted export.

Check it for identifying details.

Share only what is needed.

Email content is treated as text. Remote email images and suspicious links are not opened automatically.

06

Local analysis avoids sending submitted content to a third-party analysis service. A deployed host still receives normal website requests and connection metadata. Local processing does not guarantee anonymity or erase browser and operating-system history. Redaction is a minimisation step, not a guarantee that every identifying detail has been removed. The user should review the exported report before sharing it.

08

Position CheckFirst as education and decision support that can sit alongside an organisation's established reporting and security processes. Do not claim that it replaces an email gateway, incident response, identity verification or professional advice. A pilot should determine whether the explanations are understood and whether people know when to use the organisation's reporting route.
